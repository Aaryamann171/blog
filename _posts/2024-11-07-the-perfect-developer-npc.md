Nothing changes. I have lived the same exact life for the past 2 years. Days merge into another without break, the weekend goes by in a blink of an eye and it's monday again. Same old story, wake up, morning standup, follow the project plan, make changes, explain delays if any, update timeline, design-code-test, push to dev. Fix bugs and accidentantly add more bugs to fix later. New CR appears from the wild, refactor code, incorporate more bugs, fix bugs. Evening scrum, explain how bug fixes impacted the timeline, but it doesn't matter, the sacred project timeline shall not be altered. If a node in the sacred timeline is manipulated you must face the wrath of the stakeholders. So you put in more work, work over the weekends, work long hours to preserve the timeline. Yet you fail, only if you had planned it better, had taken more buffer time in between your delieverables and maybe, just maybe, you would have met your deadlines and maintained a healthy WLB.

Onto the next sprint, nothing changes.

Suddenly there is a glitch in the matrix. Someone accidently gave you seer powers. Your NPC character can now see into the future. Whenever you see a BRD, your visions kick in. You see the events unfolding in for the next 2 weeks. Now you can plan for all the sick leaves, unexpected bugs, requirement changes and 1000 other variables and devise a perfect plan. You wake up at 7 a.m. sharp, grab your cup of coffee and just start typing away on your amazing `Keychron K8 Pro with Brown Switches and Aluminum body`, there would be no need to test your code, you have already simulated all the edge cases in your head. You just type away and voila the module is ready. You push it to `main` directly, why waste time testing it in different enviroments. You don't even run the code locally at this point. You don't even use VS code or Vim, they are all overkill, you just edit files on the hosted repo. Much more efficient. You check off all the tasks for the next three days in your project management tool, you will develop a fever overnight, it shall not not affect the sacred timeline. You go to bed early after a healthy balanced meal to aid in your recovery. You wake up with a sore throat and a mild fever, but you knew that already, you have your kettle ready and anti-biotics on your kitchen slab. You had already mailed the stakeholders and your team members about this a week ago, so you just drop in a gentle reminder. You start to feel better with rest, but you don't overexert yourself, because it will only slow down your recovery. You take another day off and the next day, it's 7 a.m. again.
